\chapter{Deep noise suppression}
The noisy-speech distribution is tightly coupled to clean speech, making enhancement a different task from unconditional speech and image synthesis. This observation motivates diffusion- and Schr\"odinger-bridge-based models for noise suppression. This chapter investigates whether Brownian noise is needed when interpolating between paired noisy and clean data distributions. As discussed in the background chapter, Brownian motion can construct a path to a high-variance Gaussian, but paired distributions may not require the same construction.

The variance of training samples can affect model optimisation: it may regularise training, while excessive variance may make optimisation less stable. The main result is that the flow-matching model outperforms the tested SB model on additive-noise suppression. An additional investigation introduces SB-SV, showing how the SB interpolation becomes linear as $k\rightarrow1$.

A further finding is that these models perform well as direct data predictors. This questions the benefits of continuous depth, as it seems that training as a neural ODE outperforms a direct regressor, but once trained it can be treated as a direct regressor. Such behaviour connects the properties of continuous-depth training with data augmentation and curriculum learning.

The rest of this chapter explains metrics and the Voicebank-DEMAND dataset. Then specifics of the models are outlined. The results are displayed after with discussion. This chapter ends with concluding remarks.

\section{Metrics}
Speech quality is evaluated with intrusive and non-intrusive metrics. The intrusive speech-enhancement metrics are PESQ~\citep{rixPerceptualEvaluationSpeech2001}, ESTOI~\citep{jensen2016algorithm}, and \ac{SI-SDR}~\citep{leroux2018sdr}, measured in dB. The evaluation also includes non-intrusive metrics that estimate quality from the enhanced speech alone: DNSMOS~\citep{reddy2021dnsmos},\footnote{https://github.com/microsoft/DNS-Challenge/tree/master/DNSMOS} a neural predictor trained on human ratings (\ac{MOS}), and WhiSQA, a non-intrusive \ac{MOS} predictor reported to correlate with human judgments \citep{closeHallucinationPerceptualMetricDriven2024,close2025whisqanonintrusivespeechquality}.\footnote{https://github.com/leto19/WhiSQA} Higher scores indicate better quality for all metrics used here.

\section{Data}
All models are trained and tested on the \ac{VB-DMD} dataset~\citep{valentini2016investigating}, a common benchmark for \ac{SE} containing clean speech recordings from 28 speakers with added background noise, e.g. caf\'e, traffic. Speakers p226 and p287 are used for validation. Non-intrusive evaluation of the clean speech yields 3.53 DNSMOS and 4.53 WhiSQA.

\section{Model}
Here, path velocity denotes the derivative of the conditional path mean with respect to depth $u$. Static variance means variance that is independent of $u$. Data prediction (DP) and flow matching (FM) denote training objectives, whereas \ac{DDP} denotes an inference scheme.
Following \cite{jukic2024schr}, all models are trained until validation SI-SDR converges, after which the checkpoint with the best validation PESQ is selected. Unless otherwise stated, \ac{ODE} samplers are run for 50 steps, with batch size 8 and the same STFT settings as \cite{richterInvestigatingTrainingObjectives2025}.
The neural estimator $F_\theta$ employs the NCSN++ architecture \citep{song2021sde} using the same parameterisation described in \cite{richter2023speech}. All experiments use the time-domain auxiliary loss \citep{jukic2024schr}.

\section{Conclusion}
 This chapter views the depth-independence of path velocity and variance as an analogue for straightness. This chapter assessed the impact of probability path straightness on flow-based model performance for SE. The comparison of SB-VE with SB-SV showed greater improvement with depth-independent variance than with a depth-independent path velocity. Overall, speech quality metrics improved more with ICFM, which keeps both path velocity and variance independent of depth. Using depth-independent variance degraded ODE solver performance, but this can be circumvented by directly predicting the data at inference.
